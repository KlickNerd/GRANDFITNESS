What This Class Is

Boxing, minus
the bruises.

This is a technique-first boxing class, not a cardio class wearing boxing gloves. You'll learn the real fundamentals — stance, guard, footwork, the jab, the cross, hooks and defensive movement — the same way fighters learn them, drilled step by step and refined round by round. The difference: everything happens on bags, pads and in shadowboxing. Punches only ever travel one direction, and it's away from you.

What you get in return is the sport's famous side effect: fight-level fitness. Boxing's rounds structure — work hard, rest short, repeat — builds an engine, a core and a mental calm that no machine can. Members consistently call it the hardest workout they've ever loved, and the one hour a week where their head goes completely quiet.

Inside a Session

Fifty minutes,
fight-camp style.

Every class follows the rhythm boxers have trained with for a century — warm up, learn, work, empty the tank:

Min 00–10

Rope & Shadowbox

Skip rope to wake up the feet, mobilize shoulders and hips, then shadowbox the fundamentals in front of the mirror. Every boxer on earth starts this way.

Min 10–25

Technique Block

Goga breaks down the day's focus — a punch, a combination, a defensive move — demonstrates it, then drills it with you until it clicks.

Min 25–40

Bag & Pad Rounds

Timed rounds on heavy bags and focus mitts, applying today's technique with real power while Goga circles the room correcting details.

Min 40–50

The Finisher

Rounds-based conditioning: intervals, core and grip work. This is where fighter-fitness is forged — and where the class earns its reputation.

What You'll Build

Six things boxing
gives you.

Beyond the sweat, boxing quietly builds a skill set that follows you out of the gym:

Real Technique

A clean jab, sharp footwork and combinations that flow — actual boxing skill, not fitness-class choreography. You'll notice the difference by week three.

A Serious Engine

Three-minute rounds with short rests build cardiovascular fitness faster than almost anything — and it never feels like 'doing cardio'.

Explosive Power

Punching from the ground up trains legs, hips, core and shoulders as one chain. Rotational power like this transfers to every sport.

Coordination & Reflexes

Hands, feet, eyes and breathing working together — boxing is one of the best coordination workouts that exists, at any age.

Iron Core

Every punch rotates through your midsection and every defensive move braces it. Boxers don't do many crunches; they don't need to.

A Quiet Mind

You cannot think about your inbox while hitting a bag. One hour of total presence — members call it the cheapest therapy in Batumi.

Questions & Answers

Before your
first class.

Will I get hit or have to spar?

No — never in this class. Everything happens on bags, pads and in shadowboxing; punches travel one direction only. If you ever grow curious about controlled technical sparring, that's a conversation YOU start with Goga, months down the line, entirely optional.

I have zero experience and average fitness. Can I join?

Yes — most people in the room started exactly there. Every drill scales to your level, you rest as much as you need in the early weeks, and Goga teaches the fundamentals from absolute zero. Boxing is how you'll get fit, not something you get fit for.

What should I bring to my first class?

Training clothes, sports shoes and water — we'll sort you out with gear so you can simply try it. Once you're hooked, your own wraps and gloves are the first purchase; Goga will tell you exactly what's worth buying (and what isn't).

Is boxing suitable for women?

Completely — some of the most technical boxers in our classes are women. Boxing rewards speed, rhythm and precision far more than size, and everyone trains together in the same supportive room.

How fit will boxing actually make me?

Frighteningly. The rounds format builds cardiovascular fitness, power and core strength simultaneously — most members notice a different engine within a month and get comments about their shoulders by month three.

How often should I come?

Twice a week is the sweet spot for learning technique while recovering well. Once a week still works as the best conditioning session of your week — all four weekly slots are included in every membership.
