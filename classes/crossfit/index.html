What This Class Is

Fitness with
a scoreboard.

This class trains the ten dimensions of fitness at once: strength, endurance, stamina, power, speed, flexibility, coordination, agility, balance and accuracy. The method is constant variation — every day a different workout (the WOD) combining barbell lifts, bodyweight gymnastics and engine work into a session with a defined score: a time to beat, rounds to complete, a load to move. Fitness stops being vague and becomes measurable.

The magic ingredient is intensity — YOUR intensity. Every workout has a competitive-level version and a first-day version, and both people finish sweating on the same floor, cheering each other's last reps. That shared suffering builds the famous community: nowhere in the gym are names learned faster, and nowhere is the last person to finish celebrated harder. Hard, honest, together — that's the formula.

Inside a Session

One hour.
One mission.

Every class follows the same proven arc — prepare, learn, work, finish together:

Min 00–12

Warm-Up & Prep

A dynamic warm-up built around today's movements — joints prepped, heart rate up, and movement patterns rehearsed before load ever arrives.

Min 12–30

Skill & Strength

The technical block: barbell work, gymnastics progressions or a strength focus — coached in detail while everyone is fresh. This is where you get better.

The workout of the day: a scored combination of movements against the clock. Otar scales it live for every athlete — same workout, personal challenge, maximum effort.

Scores on the board, mobility work, and the round of congratulations that turns strangers into training partners. Tomorrow: something completely different.

Questions & Answers

Before your
first class.

Isn't this kind of training dangerous?

Bad coaching is dangerous — in any sport. Here, technique is taught before intensity is added, every workout is scaled to your current level, and Otar watches every rep. Train like that and functional training is not just safe: the strength and movement quality it builds actively protect you everywhere else.

Do I need to be fit before joining?

We recommend a little training base — a few weeks of general gym work or our Group Exercise class — mostly so your first WODs are fun instead of purely humbling. But 'fit'? No. Scaling means a beginner and an athlete do the same workout at completely different loads and finish equally destroyed and equally happy.

What does 'scaled' actually mean?

Every movement has easier versions: pull-ups become ring rows, barbell weights drop, distances shorten. The workout's structure and intensity stay; the difficulty adapts to you. You'll hear 'scale it' from Otar as a command, not a concession — ego-lifting is the only thing not welcome.

What's a WOD and why does everyone track scores?

Workout Of the Day — the scored centerpiece of every class. Times, rounds and loads go on the board, and benchmark workouts repeat every few months so you can measure exactly how much fitter you've become. Progress you can prove beats progress you can feel.

Will I learn Olympic lifting?

You'll learn the derivatives and progressions — cleans, presses, and the technique foundations — coached properly and loaded sensibly. Full snatch-and-jerk mastery is a longer journey Otar happily guides ambitious members through in the skill blocks.

How often should I train this?

Two to three classes a week, with rest or an easy class between them — the intensity is real and recovery is part of the program. Many members pair it with Pilates for mobility or Boxing for variety; all of it is included in your membership.
