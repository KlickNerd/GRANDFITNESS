"Won't I Get Bulky?" — 5 Myths About Women and Strength Training

No corner of fitness has collected more confident nonsense than women's training. Coach Salome has heard every myth on this list from new members — usually delivered as a reason to stay away from the weights. Here's the honest version of all five.

Myth 1: "Lifting will make me bulky"

The most persistent myth in fitness, and the most backwards. Visible "bulk" requires years of deliberate, specialized training, eating and — frankly — genetics. It cannot happen by accident, and it especially cannot happen from two or three strength sessions a week. What those sessions actually produce: shape, posture, strength and the athletic look most women describe when they say "toned".

Myth 2: "I just want to tone, so I'll use tiny weights"

Plot twist: "toned" is muscle. There is no separate toning adaptation — a firm arm is a trained muscle at a moderate body fat level. And muscles adapt to challenge, not to pink 1-kilo dumbbells waved for fifty reps. Moderate weights, honest effort, progressive overload: that's the toning program, because it's the muscle program.

Myth 3: "Cardio is for women, weights are for men"

Cardio is wonderful — for hearts of all genders. But for reshaping a body, protecting bone density (a genuinely important women's health topic from the thirties onward) and raising resting metabolism, strength training is unmatched. The best week has both. What it doesn't need is a gender label on the equipment.

Myth 4: "I shouldn't train during my period"

Energy naturally varies across the cycle — smart training varies with it, rather than stopping. Many women feel strongest in the follicular phase and dial intensity down later; gentle movement often helps symptoms more than the sofa does. Salome adjusts intensity with her members openly and matter-of-factly, because that's simply good coaching.

Myth 5: "The weight room isn't for people like me"

This one isn't answered with studies — it's answered with an environment. It's exactly why our women's fitness program exists: small-group sessions built around women, led by a female coach, with zero judgment as the house rule. Two hundred women in, Salome's favorite result is still the same: members who walk into the gym like they own the place. Because here, they do.
