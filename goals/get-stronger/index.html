Why Strength

Strong changes
everything.

Strength is the mother quality. Carrying your groceries up the stairs, playing with your kids, protecting your joints and bones as you age, standing taller in every room you enter — it all runs on strength. Build it, and every other physical goal gets easier: fat loss, muscle, sport, everyday life.

And here's the part most gyms won't tell you: getting stronger is not complicated. It's a handful of fundamental lifts, done with good technique, made slightly harder over time. The hard part is doing it consistently and correctly — and that's exactly what we're here for.

The Grand Fitness Strength Method

Four phases.
Zero guesswork.

Every member follows the same proven path — only the starting point and the speed are yours. Here's exactly what happens after you walk in:

01

Weeks 1–2

Assess & Learn

Before you touch a heavy barbell, we look at how you move. Your coach runs you through a movement screening, finds your mobility restrictions and weak links, and then teaches you the fundamental patterns — squat, hinge, press, pull — starting with an empty bar. By the end of week two you'll have clean technique and honest baseline numbers for every main lift. That baseline is your starting line: from here, everything is measured.

02

Weeks 3–8

Build the Foundation

Now the real fun starts: linear progression. Three full-body sessions per week, built around the big compound lifts — and almost every session, a little more weight goes on the bar. It sounds simple because it is: as a beginner or returning lifter, your body adapts fast, and we ride that wave for as long as it lasts. Your coach watches every set, keeps your technique honest under increasing load, and logs every number.

03

Month 3+

Progress Like an Athlete

When adding weight every session stops working, guesswork usually starts — but not here. Your program shifts to structured periodization: heavy days and volume days, rep ranges from 3 to 8, targeted accessory work for your individual weak points, and planned deload weeks so your body can absorb the work. This is exactly how competitive lifters train — scaled to your level and your life.

04

Every 8–12 Weeks

Test, Celebrate, Repeat

PR day. You test your main lifts under your coach's eyes, compare the numbers to your baseline, and watch the proof pile up: you are measurably, undeniably stronger than the person who walked in a few months ago. Then we set new targets and go again. Strength has no finish line — that's the best part.

The Science Behind It

Six principles.
No secrets.

There is no magic program and no shortcut — just training principles that have built every strong person you've ever seen. We simply apply them properly, and we never skip one.

Progressive Overload

The one law of strength: your body only adapts when the demand keeps rising. Weight, reps, sets or quality — something must progress every week. Your program makes sure it does.

Compound Lifts First

Squat, deadlift, bench, overhead press, rows. Multi-joint lifts train the most muscle, build the most strength, and carry over to real life. Machines assist — they don't lead.

Technique Is Non-Negotiable

A sloppy heavy rep builds nothing but injury risk. Every kilo you lift here is earned with clean technique first — that's why our numbers keep climbing for years, not weeks.

Recovery Is Training

Muscle grows between sessions, not during them. Your program includes rest days and deload weeks on purpose — and our sauna makes the recovery part genuinely enjoyable.

Fuel the Work

Strength is built in the kitchen too: roughly 1.6–2.2g of protein per kilo of bodyweight per day. Your coach helps you set a realistic target — no extreme diets required.

Track Everything

Every set, every session, every PR goes into your log. Numbers don't flatter and they don't lie — they show progress you can see, and they tell your coach exactly what to adjust.

Inside a Session

One hour.
Fully used.

No wandering between machines wondering what to do next. A strength session at Grand Fitness has a structure — and every minute earns its place:

Min 00–10

Warm-Up & Mobility

Raise your heart rate, open up the hips and shoulders, and groove the day's movement pattern with an empty bar. Boring? Maybe. The reason you'll lift injury-free for years? Absolutely.

Min 10–35

The Main Lift

The heart of the session: today's big lift — squat, bench, deadlift or press — for your programmed sets and weights. Your coach is right there, checking depth, bar path and bracing on every working set.

Min 35–50

Accessory Work

Two to three targeted exercises that attack your personal weak points — the muscles that limit your main lifts. This is where imbalances get fixed and plateaus get prevented.

Min 50–60

Core, Grip & Log

Finish with core and grip work — the unglamorous stuff that holds heavy weights together. Then log your numbers, compare them to last week, and leave knowing exactly what you'll beat next time.

Questions & Answers

Before you
ask.

I've never touched a barbell. Can I really start here?

Yes — beginners are our favorite lifters, because progress comes fastest in the first months. You'll start with an empty bar and a coach by your side, and you'll be amazed how quickly 'I could never squat' turns into a loaded barbell.

How fast will I actually get stronger?

Most beginners add weight to the bar almost every session for the first two to three months — it's the famous 'newbie gains' window and we make the most of it. After that, progress becomes steadier but never stops: our members set new PRs years into training.

I'm a woman — won't lifting make me bulky?

No — that's the most persistent myth in fitness. Strength training makes you strong, athletic and resilient; getting 'bulky' requires years of deliberate, specialized effort. Many of our strongest members are women, and Coach Salome runs dedicated women's sessions if you'd rather start there.

Am I too old to start strength training?

The opposite: strength training is one of the best things you can do as you age. Muscle and bone density respond to training at every age — we simply adjust the starting point and progression speed. Our oldest members are living proof.

How often do I need to train?

Three sessions per week is the sweet spot for strength — enough stimulus to progress, enough recovery to grow. Each session takes about an hour. If you can only manage two, we'll build a program around that; consistency beats perfection.

Do I need a personal trainer, or can I train alone?

Both work here. Your free first session includes a coach who sets up your program, and our floor coaches keep an eye on technique even when you train solo. If you want the fastest possible progress, 1-on-1 sessions with Gurgen or the team are available anytime.
