  title: "Four phases.<br>Visible results."
  lead: "Every muscle-building member follows the same proven path — your starting point and your pace are the only variables. Here's the road ahead:"

    - when: "Weeks 1–2"
      title: "Assess & Calibrate"
      text: "We start by finding your true starting point: training history, movement quality, and how your body responds to volume.       title: "Build the Volume Base"
      text: "Now we build. You'll train a structured full-body or upper/lower split, and week by week your working volume — the total hard sets per muscle group — climbs carefully.       title: "Specialize & Periodize"

      title: "Measure, Compare, Adjust"

faq:
  eyebrow: "Questions & Answers"
  title: "Before you<br>ask."
  items:
    - q: "How long until I actually see muscle?"
      a: "You'll feel changes — fuller muscles, better pumps, clothes fitting differently — within 4–8 weeks. Visible changes others notice usually take 3–6 months of consistent training. A genuinely transformed physique is a 12-month project. We're honest about this because honest timelines are the ones people actually finish."
    - q: "Do I need supplements?"
      a: "No — food first, always. That said, two things are worth knowing: a protein powder is a convenient way to hit your daily target (not magic, just convenient), and creatine monohydrate is the single best-researched supplement in existence. Your coach will tell you the same thing the science says — and nothing more."
    - q: "Can I build muscle and lose fat at the same time?"
      a: "If you're new to training, returning after a break, or carrying extra body fat — yes, body recomposition is very real, and it's the most rewarding phase in training. Advanced lifters usually alternate building and defining phases. Your coach will place you honestly."
    - q: "Do I have to be sore to make progress?"
      a: "No. Soreness measures novelty, not growth. Your real progress metric is the logbook: more weight or more reps at the same quality, week over week. Some of your best growth weeks will barely leave you sore at all."
    - q: "How heavy should I train?"
      a: "Mostly in the 6–12 rep range, taking sets close to — but rarely to — failure. Heavy enough to matter, controlled enough to grow from and recover from. Your coach calibrates this with you in your first weeks; after that, the logbook keeps everyone honest."
    - q: "Which training split is best?"
      a: "The one you'll actually show up for. Three full-body days, upper/lower, push/pull/legs — they all work when volume and effort are right. We build your split around your real schedule, not around an influencer's."
