Walk into any gym and you'll see it: someone racing to drink a protein shake within minutes of their last set, convinced the "anabolic window" is slamming shut. The truth is both more relaxed and more useful — and it changes what you should actually focus on.

## Total daily protein comes first

Before timing matters at all, the total has to be right. For people training regularly, research consistently supports roughly **1.6–2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day**. For an 80 kg member, that's around 130–175 g daily — noticeably more than most people eat by default, and the single biggest nutrition upgrade most lifters can make.

## Is the anabolic window real?

Sort of — but it's more of an anabolic *barn door*. Muscle protein synthesis stays elevated for many hours after training, not thirty minutes. If you ate a protein-containing meal within a few hours before your session, your body is still digesting and delivering those amino acids while you train and after. The post-workout shake isn't wrong; it's just not the emergency it's marketed as.

## What timing actually does matter

- **Spread protein across the day.** Aim for 3–5 meals with 25–40 g of protein each. Regular doses beat one giant dinner because each protein-rich meal triggers its own wave of muscle protein synthesis.
- **Bracket your training.** A protein-rich meal within a few hours before *or* after your session covers the "window" comfortably. Train fasted in the morning? Then a post-workout meal soon after becomes more useful.
- **Don't skip protein before bed.** A slow-digesting protein source in the evening — dairy is the classic — supports overnight recovery, when much of your actual adaptation happens.

## The Grand Fitness bottom line

Get the daily total right, spread it across the day, and put one solid protein meal somewhere near your training. Do that consistently and the stopwatch can stay in your pocket.
